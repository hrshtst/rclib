    Reward-Modulated Hebbian Learning

    The readouts of the other theory pages learn from targets: they are told the output they should have produced. Reward-modulated Hebbian learning needs much less. The model only receives a scalar reward \(R\) that says how good its recent behaviour was, and learns by exploring: it perturbs its own activity and correlates the perturbations with the reward. No target, no error per output and no gradient is given, so the same rule can learn goal-directed behaviour in a closed loop with an environment, a body or a user.

    rclib implements one composable rule, learning.RewardModulatedHebbian, that covers the variants of four papers (see References), and two places where it can learn: the readout (readouts.RewardModulated) and the recurrent weights of a reservoir (reservoirs.RewardModulated).

    The Rule

    As everywhere in rclib, activities are row vectors and a weight matrix \(\mathbf{W}\) maps a pre-synaptic row \(\mathbf{p}\) to the post-synaptic potential \(\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{p}\mathbf{W}\), so \(W_{ij}\) is the weight from unit \(i\) to unit \(j\). Here \(\mathbf{p}\) is the activity on the input side of the weights that learn, not necessarily the model's input. For a reward-modulated readout it is the feature row the readout receives: the reservoir state, with a trailing 1 when the readout has a bias. For a reward-modulated reservoir it is the previous rate \(\mathbf{r}(t-1)\) of its own neurons, because only its recurrent weights learn; its input and bias add fixed terms to the potential (see Where the Rule Learns). The model's input is \(\mathbf{p}\) only when an identity NVAR passes it straight to a reward-modulated readout.

    While exploring, every post-synaptic unit adds noise \(\boldsymbol{\xi}\) to its potential. Each exploring step computes a post-synaptic signal \(\mathbf{q}\) and adds the Hebbian product of the two sides to an eligibility \(\mathbf{E}\):

    \[ \mathbf{E} \leftarrow \gamma \mathbf{E} + S(\mathbf{p})^{T} S(\mathbf{q}), \qquad S(y) = \operatorname{sign}(y)\,|y|^{k} \]

    with \(\gamma\) the trace_decay of online updates (\(\gamma = 1\) within a trial, which clears \(\mathbf{E}\) at its update) and \(k\) the eligibility_power. Since \(S(ab) = S(a)S(b)\), the term is an outer product. The post-synaptic signal is one of

    • the noise itself, \(\mathbf{q} = \boldsymbol{\xi}\) (post="noise"), when it is known;
    • the potential minus its average before this step, \(\mathbf{q} = \mathbf{v} - \bar{\mathbf{v}}\), with \(\bar{\mathbf{v}} \leftarrow d\,\bar{\mathbf{v}} + (1 - d)\,\mathbf{v}\) (post="high_pass", \(d\) the post_decay; \(d = 0\) gives the first difference of the potential);
    • the potential itself (post="potential").

    A reward then changes the weights in proportion to the eligibility and a modulator \(m\):

    \[ \Delta \mathbf{W} = \eta_n\, m\, \mathbf{E} \qquad \text{or, decorrelated,} \qquad \Delta \mathbf{W} = \eta_n\, m\, (\mathbf{C} + \lambda \mathbf{I})^{-1} \mathbf{E} \]

    where \(\mathbf{C}\) sums \(\mathbf{p}^{T}\mathbf{p}\) over the trial, \(\lambda\) is the decorrelation, and \(\eta_n = \eta / (1 + n/T)\) decays with the number \(n\) of updates when learning_rate_decay_steps \(T\) is set. The modulator compares the reward with a baseline \(\bar{R}\), kept per integer key such as a trial type, before the reward is added to it:

    \[ m = R - \bar{R} \quad (\texttt{"difference"}) \qquad \text{or} \qquad m = \begin{cases} 1 & R > \bar{R} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \quad (\texttt{"heaviside"}) \]

    The baseline is an exponential average of the key's rewards, the mean of the last few, zero, a value the caller passes (any critic), or bypassed entirely by giving \(m\) directly with ESN.modulate. The first reward of a key with an internal baseline becomes its own baseline, so its modulator is zero.

    Why It Works

    Write the reward as a function of the potentials and suppose the noise is small, zero-mean and independent across units and steps, with variance \(\sigma^2\). Then, to first order, \(R - \bar{R} \approx \sum_j \frac{\partial R}{\partial v_j} \xi_j\), and the expected change of a weight under \(\mathbf{q} = \boldsymbol{\xi}\), \(k = 1\) is

    \[ \mathbb{E}[\Delta W_{ij}] = \eta\, \mathbb{E}\!\left[p_i\, \xi_j\, (R - \bar{R})\right] \approx \eta\, \sigma^{2}\, p_i \frac{\partial R}{\partial v_j} = \eta\, \sigma^{2} \frac{\partial R}{\partial W_{ij}} . \]

    The rule climbs the gradient of the reward without computing it: this is node perturbation. The other choices make it work with less information or in harder conditions:

    • The baseline removes the part of the reward that does not depend on the noise. Without it the updates have a large bias and variance, which can make learning collapse.
    • The high-pass signal estimates noise the synapse cannot observe: when the inputs change slowly, the potential minus its recent average is mostly the noise. This is the exploratory Hebbian rule, local to the synapse.
    • The supralinear eligibility (\(k = 3\)) helps when the reward comes only at the end of a trial. A kick to a recurrent network's potential is followed by a long, small relaxation that adds unrelated terms to the eligibility; the cube keeps the few large products of the kicks and suppresses the many small ones.
    • The Heaviside modulator learns only from steps better than expected, a coarser but robust signal.
    • Decorrelation preconditions the update with the inverse covariance of the pre-synaptic activity, which matters when inputs are redundant, correlated or on different scales. Its step is normalized by the trial's covariance, so its learning rates are not comparable with the plain rule's.

    Published Variants

    The presets in rclib.learning set the choices that define each variant; the learning rate and the exploration amplitude depend on the task, and every setting can be overridden. They are starting points, not reproductions of the papers.

    Preset Learns Exploration Post signal Eligibility Modulator, baseline Update
    legenstein2010 the layer feeding the explored units uniform, every unit and step high-pass, \(d = 0.8\) linear difference, EMA 0.8 every rewarded step
    hoerzer2014 a readout whose noisy output is fed back uniform, every step first difference linear Heaviside, EMA 0.8 every step, decaying rate
    miconi2017 recurrent weights sparse kicks (probability 0.003, amplitude 0.5) high-pass, \(d = 0.05\) cube difference, EMA 0.33 per trial type once per trial, clipped
    burms2015 recurrent weights or sensor feedback Gaussian 0.05, every step the known noise linear difference, mean of the last 50 per input once per trial, decorrelated

    Where the Rule Learns

    A reward-modulated readout adds the noise to its outputs, \(\mathbf{z} = \mathbf{x}\mathbf{W} + \boldsymbol{\xi}\), where \(\mathbf{x}\) holds the features (and a trailing 1 with a bias). To feed the output back into the reservoir, as Hoerzer et al. do, append it to the next input: since the input weights are drawn uniformly from \([-s, s]\) with \(s\) the input_scaling, the appended columns act as feedback weights with the same distribution. With an identity NVAR (Nvar(num_lags=1, polynomial_order=1)) the readout learns directly from observations, for example from the sensors of a body that plays the role of the reservoir.

    A reward-modulated reservoir learns its recurrent weights. Its neurons integrate a potential,

    \[ \mathbf{v}(t) = (1 - \alpha)\,\mathbf{v}(t-1) + \alpha\left(\mathbf{u}(t)\mathbf{W}_{in} + \mathbf{b} + \mathbf{r}(t-1)\mathbf{W}\right) + \boldsymbol{\xi}(t), \qquad \mathbf{r}(t) = \tanh \mathbf{v}(t), \]

    with the noise outside the leak, so that a kick stays in the dynamics for about \(1/\alpha\) steps. The pre-synaptic activity is the previous rate \(\mathbf{r}(t-1)\) and the post-synaptic one the potential. Learning changes only existing synapses, and optional lists of plastic pre- and post-synaptic neurons keep the rest fixed, for example the synapses of designated output neurons. With \(\alpha = 1\) and no noise the reservoir equals a RandomSparse reservoir with the same weights.
